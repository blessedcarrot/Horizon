### [AI in Drug Discovery: Assessing the Real Progress Behind the Hype](https://www.science.org/content/blog-post/so-how-ai-drug-discovery-doing-really) ⭐️ 7.0/10

This item centers on a Nature paper examining the actual state of AI in drug discovery, discussed via Derek Lowe&\#x27;s commentary on Science.org \(the original link was reportedly swapped from the free Science.org version to a paywalled Elsevier version, then restored\). The analysis pushes back against inflated expectations of AI delivering novel cures, instead framing its current value as accelerating existing workflows rather than enabling fundamentally new capabilities. This is reinforced by a Hacker News commenter, a structural biologist at a mid-sized biotech, who describes using AI tools daily for tasks like installing academic software, debugging code, writing scripts to query large datasets, and reviewing experiment drafts—speeding up familiar work without unlocking previously impossible outcomes. The discussion contrasts this grounded, practitioner-level view with broader public narratives that expect AI to produce dramatic breakthroughs like cure-all treatments.

**「Background」** Derek Lowe is a longtime medicinal chemist who has written the widely respected &\#x27;In the Pipeline&\#x27; blog on pharmaceutical drug discovery since 2002, making his commentary a trusted lens for interpreting new academic findings in the field. His writing here discusses a Nature-published paper assessing AI&\#x27;s real-world track record in drug discovery, following earlier work in which he compared clinical trial success rates of AI-discovered versus conventionally discovered drugs and found little meaningful difference. This context matters because AI in drug discovery has been heavily hyped, while actual outcomes such as Phase 1 and 2 trial pass rates offer a more measurable way to judge its impact.

**「Impact」** For biotech practitioners and researchers, the piece reinforces that AI&\#x27;s near-term value lies in productivity gains for existing workflows rather than in generating novel therapeutic breakthroughs, which may temper investment expectations and hype cycles in the sector.

**「Community Discussion」** Commenters largely agree that AI&\#x27;s practical impact in drug discovery today is incremental efficiency rather than transformative new capability, as illustrated by a structural biologist&\#x27;s day-to-day use cases; one commenter offered a counterpoint, arguing that AI&\#x27;s biggest impact may be democratizing access for patients and non-experts lacking formal training or resources, citing a self-built patient-facing tool as an example.
