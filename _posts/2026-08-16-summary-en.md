### [Derek Lowe assesses the real state of AI in drug discovery](https://www.science.org/content/blog-post/so-how-ai-drug-discovery-doing-really) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Chemist and longtime industry blogger Derek Lowe discusses a Nature Reviews Drug Discovery paper that assesses where AI actually stands in drug discovery, cutting through hype to look at concrete outcomes rather than promotional claims. The piece argues that despite years of bold predictions about AI revolutionizing pharmaceutical R&amp;D, the field has yet to produce transformative breakthroughs like dramatically faster approvals or novel drug classes discovered purely through AI. Instead, the paper and Lowe&\#x27;s commentary point to a more modest reality: AI tools are proving useful for specific, well-defined tasks within the discovery pipeline, such as data analysis, molecule screening assistance, and workflow acceleration, without fundamentally changing what is scientifically achievable. The analysis emphasizes that separating genuine incremental progress from marketing narratives is essential for setting realistic expectations about AI&\#x27;s near-term role in pharma.

**「Background」** AI in drug discovery refers to using machine learning methods, such as protein structure prediction and generative molecule design, to speed up steps like target identification, compound screening, and lead optimization, areas long dominated by slow, expensive trial-and-error experimentation. Derek Lowe, a longtime medicinal chemist and blogger at Science.org&\#x27;s &\#x27;In the Pipeline,&\#x27; is a well-known industry commentator who frequently offers grounded, practitioner-level critiques of pharma hype cycles. His post discusses a peer-reviewed paper in Nature Reviews Drug Discovery that assesses the field&\#x27;s actual progress and makes recommendations for researchers and AI companies going forward.

**「Impact」** For biotech practitioners and investors, the analysis reinforces that AI in drug discovery should currently be evaluated as a productivity and efficiency tool rather than a source of scientific breakthroughs, which may temper expectations tied to AI-driven biotech valuations and timelines.
