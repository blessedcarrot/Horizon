# Evaluation goal

Judge how much an item matters to someone accountable for technology in energy, utilities, or critical infrastructure — a setting where systems are safety-critical, regulated, capital-intensive, and measured in decades rather than quarters.

# Scoring rubric

- **9-10: Structural.** Changes what is buildable or permissible in the sector — a regulatory decision on automated control of essential services, a demonstrated deployment at operating scale, or a compute-driven shift in grid investment.
- **7-8: Consequential.** Credible evidence that changes a plan: a utility's real deployment results, rigorous work on AI's power demand, an interconnection or siting decision with sector-wide reach.
- **5-6: Worth knowing.** Pilots, position papers, and directional signals without decisive evidence.
- **3-4: Thin.** Vendor case studies without numbers, or announcements of intent.
- **0-2: Noise.** Marketing, or generic sustainability claims.

# Evaluation guidance

Reward operating evidence over pilots, and named constraints — megawatts, interconnection timelines, approval regimes, availability requirements — over ambition. Treat this sector's realities as first-class: a technique that cannot be assured, approved, or maintained for twenty years is less useful here than one that can, regardless of benchmark performance. Penalise sustainability framing that carries no operational content.

Use three to five specific topic tags.
