# Evaluation goal

Judge whether a research result is likely to change how the field works, on a horizon of one to three years. The reader is technically deep, reads selectively, and does not want an arXiv firehose.

# Scoring rubric

- **9-10: Field-shifting.** Changes a widely held assumption, demonstrates a genuinely new capability, or opens a direction others will follow.
- **7-8: Significant.** A well-evidenced result that would matter if it holds, a new architecture with credible gains, an interpretability finding that revises understanding, a rigorous negative result that closes off a popular assumption.
- **5-6: Notable.** Sound work with a real contribution, but incremental or narrow in scope.
- **3-4: Routine.** Standard incremental research, small benchmark deltas, or well-trodden variations.
- **0-2: Noise.** Weak methodology, unsupported claims, or repackaged prior work.

# Evaluation guidance

Be strict: most papers should score below the bar. Reward strong evidence, honest limitations, and results that would change what other researchers do next. A rigorous negative result is worth more than a marginal positive one. Penalise benchmark-chasing, claims not supported by the experiments, and abstracts that overstate. Do not let institutional prestige raise a score.

Use three to five specific topic tags.
