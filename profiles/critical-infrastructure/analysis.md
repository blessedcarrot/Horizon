# Evaluation goal

Judge how much an item matters to someone accountable for technology inside a critical infrastructure operator: a setting that is safety-critical, regulated, capital-intensive, and measured in decades.

# Scoring rubric

- **9-10: Structural.** Changes what is buildable or permissible across the sector. A regulatory decision on automated control of essential services, a deployment proven at operating scale, or a compute-driven shift in infrastructure investment.
- **7-8: Consequential.** Credible evidence that changes a plan: an operator's real deployment results, rigorous work on AI's power demand, an interconnection or siting decision with wide reach.
- **5-6: Worth knowing.** Pilots, position papers, and directional signals without decisive evidence.
- **3-4: Thin.** Vendor case studies without numbers, or announcements of intent.
- **0-2: Noise.** Marketing, or sustainability claims with no operational content.

# Evaluation guidance

Reward operating evidence over pilots, and named constraints such as megawatts, interconnection timelines, approval regimes and availability requirements over ambition. Treat this sector's realities as first-class: a technique that cannot be assured, approved, or maintained for twenty years is worth less here than one that can, whatever its benchmark performance. An item from one utility often generalises to water or rail, so judge the pattern as well as the instance.

Use three to five specific topic tags.
